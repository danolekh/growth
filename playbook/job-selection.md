# Job Selection — what to pursue, what to skip

Don't spray proposals. Pursue jobs where Dan has a real edge and the client is worth working for.

## CURRENT PHASE: profile-building (as of 2026-07-18)

One completed job, one 5.0 review, **no JSS yet** — JSS appears after a 2nd outcome from a 2nd
client, and the first 2–3 contracts disproportionately weight the initial score. So right now the
product being bought is **a 5-star review from a new client**, and job selection optimizes for that:

- **Sweet spot: $200–800 fixed, crisp scope, deliverable in under 2 weeks.** Small enough to be
  highly winnable and fast to complete, big enough to weigh in JSS and filter out bottom-feeder
  clients. Bigger jobs aren't banned — a great $1.5k+ fit is still a pursue — but they don't get
  priority over a certain quick win anymore.
- **Avoid <$100 gigs and cheap-client posts** ($5 logo-mill energy): they attract the client type
  most likely to leave bad private feedback, which poisons an early JSS.
- **The niche priority lane (see below) outranks everything else.**
- Repeat work from existing clients (Ashraf) = great money + JSS long-term-relationship weight, but
  it won't trigger JSS — that needs a **new** client.

## The niche priority lane — performance / site rescue

Dan's strongest proof is the Oasi Kadir result: **mobile PageSpeed 69 → 99, load ~16s → under 2s**,
stated verbatim in a 5.0 review, with a public case study (https://www.danolekh.com/p/oasi-kadir).
Jobs where that proof is devastating get a fast-track:

- **Shape:** "my site is slow", PageSpeed/Lighthouse/Core Web Vitals fixes, site speed optimization,
  hosting/DNS/site migrations, "rescue my broken site" — on Next.js / Astro / React / Strapi /
  headless-CMS stacks (WordPress-adjacent is fine when the fix is frontend/hosting-level, not deep
  PHP plugin work).
- **Scoring:** these jobs get **Winnability +1** (cap 5) and default to **pursue** when total ≥ 12
  and no dimension = 1 — the before/after numbers + review + case study out-proof nearly any
  competitor at this size.
- **No new demo needed:** the "demo" is the case study + review + numbers. Lead with those
  (`../me/portfolio.md`).
- These clients leave great reviews because the result is a number they can see.

## Green flags (pursue)
- **Clear, well-defined scope** — wireframes, content models, schemas provided (like Oasi Kadir).
- **Assets ready** — client provides content, design, brand. "You execute" jobs suit Dan.
- **Defined budget**, ideally milestone-based or open to it.
- **Stack matches** `me/skills.md` — Astro, React, Next.js, Strapi, Tailwind, Drizzle, Postgres, TS.
- **Demo-able deliverable** — something concrete a few-hour demo can showcase (landing page, dashboard).
- **Healthy client signals** — money spent, prior hires, reviews/rating, payment verified.
- **Fresh post, low competition** — posted <24h with few proposals. Speed-to-apply beats boosting:
  a targeted proposal in the first hour sits at the top of the client's screen organically.
- Reasonable timeline (weeks, not "yesterday").

## Red flags (skip or be cautious)
- **Vague scope** — "build me a website/app" with no detail; high scope-creep risk.
- **Hype language** — "rockstar / ninja / wizard", "must be a genius", unrealistic combos.
- **No budget / equity-only / "exposure"** / pay clearly below the scope (a $100 "build me a full
  app"). *Small is fine — underpaid-for-scope is the flag.* A $300 job with $300 worth of scope is
  exactly what this phase wants.
- **Stale + saturated** — posted 3+ days ago with 40+ proposals and no hire: either the client
  isn't active or nothing has satisfied them; the proposal joins a graveyard.
- **Design-from-scratch required** — Dan executes designs, he's not a brand/visual designer. Skip
  unless they supply the design, or scope it as dev-only.
- **Unrealistic urgency** — large scope + tiny deadline.
- **Brand-new client, poor signals** — $0 spent, no reviews, unverified payment, evasive on details.
- **Stack mismatch** — WordPress-heavy, PHP/Laravel, mobile-native, etc. (not Dan's core).

## Scoring rubric (rate each 1–5)
| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Skills fit** | How well does it map to `me/skills.md`? |
| **Winnability** | Clear scope? Strong proof to lead with (niche lane = +1)? Fresh post? Can Dan stand out fast? |
| **Value** | **Review-per-effort, not raw budget.** A certain, cleanly-scoped $200–800 job finished in days scores 5. A big vague $5k build with weeks of risk scores 2–3 *in this phase*. Fair pay for the scope is the floor, not the ceiling. |
| **Client quality** | Spend history, reviews, hire rate, payment verified, communication. Weighted extra now: this person writes the next review. |

**Verdict:**
- **Total ≥ 15 and no dimension = 1** → *pursue*. If Winnability is high via a demo → *pursue-with-demo*.
- **Niche-lane job with total ≥ 12 and no dimension = 1** → *pursue* (the case-study numbers are the demo).
- **10–14** → pursue only if a sharp angle or quick demo tips it.
- **< 10 or any hard red flag** → *skip*; spend the proposal credit elsewhere.

## Why this phase exists (the compounding math)
A few clean 5-star wins from different clients unlock, in order: **JSS** (2nd client outcome, likely
near 100% given the current 5.0) → **Rising Talent** (profile completeness is the other gate) →
**Top Rated** (90% JSS held 13 of 16 weeks; the earnings and 90-day gates are already met). Each tier
visibly upgrades the search card and invitation flow. The Oasi Kadir client started at $0 spent /
0 reviews but had a crisp scope and responded well — clear scope + responsive client is still worth
more than a big budget with a vague brief. Full ladder + checkboxes: `../me/account-fixes.md`.
