# The Trust Gap — why shortlisted proposals don't get messaged

Distilled from the **QR-ordering SaaS loss** (`../applications/_archive/qr-ordering-saas/proposal.md`): a strong
proposal *with a working live demo* on the client's exact stack got **viewed but never messaged**. Out
of 106 proposals the client messaged 3. This doc is about winning that *last* gate.

## The funnel
```
unopened  →  opened  →  shortlisted  →  MESSAGED
```
- A **demo / exact-stack proof wins the OPEN** (and the shortlist). It got Dan into the top ~15–24% of
  106 — it works.
- **Reviews + fast response time + boost + de-risk framing + an on-target profile win the MESSAGE.**
  A demo alone can't carry this gate.

So "viewed but not messaged" usually isn't a weak proposal — it's a *trust* shortfall at the final step.

## The trust gap for a young account
While the account is young (few jobs, **no JSS, ~no reviews**), the demo proves **"can he build it"** —
but **not** "is he reliable, communicates well, and finishes." That second question is exactly what
**reviews** answer, and it's what a nervous client buys.

It bites hardest with **$0-spent / first-time-hiring clients**: they can't judge code themselves, so
they lean almost entirely on review counts and JSS to de-risk a paid build. (The QR client was
verified but **$0 spent** — a first-timer.) Counter it two ways: **bank reviews fast**
(`../me/account-fixes.md`) and **de-risk inside the proposal, up front** (`proposals.md` → "Neutralize
the client's #1 fear early").

## Read the Insights panel before drawing conclusions
- The funnel counts (unopened/opened/shortlisted/messaged) tell you **which gate you lost**. Lost at
  *open*? → hook/visibility/boost problem. Lost at *message* despite being opened? → trust gap, this doc.
- The bid averages (**all / messaged / top-rated**) tell you what the client is buying. On the QR job:
  **messaged $28/hr vs all $25.10 vs top-rated $22.98** — the messaged group bid *higher* than average.
  **The client was buying confidence, not cheapness — underbidding would have hurt, not helped.** When
  *messaged ≥ all*, hold or raise your number; don't discount.

## The saturation rule
The demo edge **decays as the proposal count climbs**. On a 100+ proposal job the message rate is
brutal (here ~3/106 ≈ **2.8%**), and the shortlist often fills before an organic proposal is even read.

- **Prioritize fresh / low-competition jobs** (roughly **< 20 proposals**) — that's where the demo lands
  while attention is high and a boost buys a top slot cheaply (see `connects.md`).
- On a saturated job, only go in if you'll **boost to the top-4** *and* have a genuine differentiator —
  otherwise spend the connects on a fresher post.

## The opposite of this loss
The **Oasi Kadir win** (`../case-studies/oasi-kadir.md`) was a *fresh, well-scoped, responsive* client —
demo + boost converted fast. The QR job was *saturated + $0-spent + risk-averse*. Same demo, opposite
outcome. The variable wasn't proposal quality; it was **competition density × client risk-appetite**.
Pick jobs accordingly.
